# ADR-0021: Convention travel rider: UN 38.3 evidence and 30% state of charge for flying props

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-19
- **Persona:** ⚡ Piko (lead), 🦊 Coco (records)
- **Scope:** org-wide

## Context

ADR-0013 gates how worn cells are sourced. Getting them to a
convention is governed by different law: UN 38.3 transport testing,
plus the UN Model Regulations changes in force since January 1, 2026,
which make the 30% state-of-charge limit binding also for lithium
batteries packed with equipment, and require manufacturers' UN 38.3
test summaries to be available along the supply chain. The forge's
posture is attendee at MomoCon 2027 (Atlanta), flying with worn
Vessels whose batteries already pass the ADR-0013 gate. Airports,
however, do not read forge doctrine.

## Decision

1. Any prop or Vessel flying with its cells travels with a **travel
   docket**, filed next to the ADR-0014 risk docket closure for the
   same event: per-cell and per-pack vendor test summaries (UN 38.3),
   the battery configuration of each traveling item, and a
   pre-departure checklist certifying state of charge at or under 30%
   and carry-on carriage.
2. Spare batteries ride carry-on only, terminals protected; worn
   Vessels ride as packed equipment under the same 2026 rules, so the
   30% discipline applies on the flight out, with charging on site.
3. The travel docket is a record class per ADR-0015: retained per event
   plus one year.
4. Cells that cannot produce test summaries do not fly, regardless of
   how loved they are; the bench keeps them home.

## Consequences

- Airport and airline conversations become document exchanges instead
  of negotiations; the 2026 rules are met by evidence, not charm.
- Cost: one careful preparation afternoon per convention trip, plus
  asking vendors for test summaries at purchase time (which feeds the
  ADR-0013 BOM gate rather than competing with it).
- Blast radius: all traveling builds; MomoCon 2027 is the first
  affected trip.
