# ADR-0014: Risk assessment docket: ISO 12100 hazard register for towers and worn Vessels

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-11-19
- **Persona:** 🔨 Zaku (lead, towers), ✨ Nui (worn surfaces), ⚡ Piko (electrical hazards)
- **Scope:** org-wide

## Context

The forge runs laser modules (up to 40 W), heated extrusion, solvents
and resins, and puts electronics on the Architect's body near crowds.
Hazards are handled by instinct and craft sense, which is real but
private. ISO 12100:2010 is the international method: identify hazards,
estimate and evaluate risk, reduce it in a defined order
(inherently safe design first, then safeguards, then information),
and document the whole. Its habit converts instinct into evidence.
ISO 31000 supplies the principles wrapper for how risk decisions are
recorded and weighed, deliberately guidance, never a checklist.

## Decision

1. Each hazard domain carries a **risk assessment docket in the
   ISO 12100 format**: (a) the tower fleet and its integrations,
   a standing docket in the hub; (b) each worn Vessel or prop, its own
   docket housed in its project repo and closed before any convention
   debut.
2. Dockets identify hazards, estimate severity and likelihood, record
   chosen reductions in the 12100 order, and name the open residuals
   honestly.
3. Risk decisions that wait on judgment are recorded "pending the
   Architect's word", never silently settled, matching both ISO 31000
   principles and Ledger law.
4. The first artifacts scheduled: the tower docket, and the Kogane
   pre-MomoCon docket (includes worn-hazard review with garmenting).

## Consequences

- Gear that leaves the pocket dimension carries a document that
  stands up outside it; venues, insurers, and future collaborators
  read evidence instead of trusting charm.
- Cost: a careful afternoon per docket, and honest residuals on paper.
- Blast radius: hub (tower docket) plus every wearable project repo
  before its debut.
